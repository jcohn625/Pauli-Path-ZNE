# Literature map: message passing, PEPS algorithms and Apple execution

Reviewed 13 October 2026. This complements the [measured exploration](apple-deep-exploration.md) and the earlier [GPU tensor-network review](gpu-tensor-network-literature.md). Sources are papers, author repositories and official documentation. “Full text” below means relevant algorithm/limitation sections were inspected, not that every theorem or implementation was independently reproduced. Recent preprints are identified explicitly. Recommendations in the applicability columns are our analysis.

## Message passing and finite-network contraction

| Source | Evidence inspected | Relevance to our signed Pauli PEPS |
|---|---|---|
| Alkabetz & Arad, [Tensor Networks contraction and the Belief Propagation algorithm](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.04433), PR Research 2021 | Abstract and publication metadata | Connects simple-update mean-field environments with BP. Adding plain BP is not automatically adding the loop information missing from simple update. |
| Guo, Poletti & Arad, [Block belief propagation algorithm for two-dimensional tensor networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2301.05844), PRB 2023 | Abstract | Blocks retain internal correlations and permit parallel updates. Finite/infinite Heisenberg demonstrations are relevant, but physical-state norm contraction differs from our signed scalar representation. |
| Wang et al., [Tensor Network Message Passing](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.01874), 2023 preprint | Abstract | Combines exact local subgraphs with messages on a sparse larger graph. Its globally tree-like, locally dense regime suggests graph partitioning; a square PEPS has loops at multiple scales. |
| Tindall & Fishman, [Gauging tensor networks with belief propagation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.17837), SciPost 2023, manuscript revised 2025 | Abstract and metadata | BP-based gauges can improve conditioning and simple update. Gauge improvement should be evaluated separately from contraction accuracy; preserving charge blocks is essential. |
| Evenbly et al., [Loop Series Expansions for Tensor Networks](https://arxiv.org/abs/2409.03108), manuscript 2024 | Abstract | Expand around BP by explicitly including loop contributions. Useful if small loop corrections dominate. Enumerating all relevant loops can negate the gain. |
| Midha & Zhang, [Beyond Belief Propagation: Cluster-Corrected Tensor Network Contraction with Exponential Convergence](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02290), PRX Quantum 7, 033010 (2026) | Abstract and publisher record | Exponential convergence requires sufficiently decaying loop contributions. It is not a guarantee for every signed network or every finite-noise circuit. |
| Gray et al., [Tensor Network Loop Cluster Expansions for Quantum Many-Body Problems](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05647), PRB 113, 235135 (2026) | Full-text overview and abstract | Supports a practical hierarchy of selected cluster corrections, including finite boundaries. Ground-state examples motivate a pilot, not direct accuracy claims for real-time noisy Pauli dynamics. |
| Midha et al., [Belief Propagation and Tensor Network Expansions for Many-Body Quantum Systems: Rigorous Results and Fundamental Limits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.03228), April 2026 preprint | Abstract | Relates loop-decay assumptions to exponential decay of connected correlations; warns against extrapolation to critical regimes. Test loop contributions and environment convergence instead of assuming noise proves decay. |
| Sim et al., [Stochastic Loop Corrections to Belief Propagation for Tensor Network Contraction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2603.08427), March 2026 preprint | Abstract | Stochastic loop evaluation may avoid exhaustive enumeration, but the demonstrated pairwise symmetric-potential/Ising setting is narrower than our signed network. Mixing, sign cancellation and relative error remain costs. |
| Tindall, Sommers & Kappen, [Contracting Tensor Networks with Generalized Belief Propagation](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.24760v1), April 2026 preprint | Full text: update equations, positivity, signed-entry limitations | Overlapping regions can capture more loops than disjoint blocks. Fractional message powers and loss of PSD preservation complicate signed inputs. A small plaquette pilot is justified; a production replacement is not. |
| Woolls et al., [Matrix Product Belief Propagation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.05598), September 2026 preprint | Full text: finite estimator, small-error expansion and stationarity | Keeps correlated boundary messages rather than product messages. We tested the inexpensive two-sided estimator; full stationary updates remain a separate next step. |
| Manabe & Pan, [CT-bMPS: Approximate Tensor Network Contraction with Boundary MPS on Contraction Trees](https://arxiv.org/html/2610.06574v1), October 2026 preprint | Full text: construction, environment compression, signed benchmarks, limitations | Particularly close to finite observable contraction. MPS boundaries avoid storing dense tree separators, but bidirectional message storage still grows with the number and length of separators. Supports testing environment-guided compression before arbitrary trees. |
| Gray & Chan, [Hyper-optimized approximate contraction of tensor networks with arbitrary geometry](https://arxiv.org/abs/2206.07044), PRX 2024 | Abstract and method overview | Optimizes contraction/compression order with approximate environments. Potentially important for anisotropic or irregular hardware graphs; changed truncation locations require corresponding residual records. |
| Gray & Kourtis, [Hyper-optimized tensor network contraction](https://quantum-journal.org/papers/q-2021-03-15-410/), Quantum 2021 | Earlier review and optimizer documentation | Cache contraction trees for repeated shapes, optimize memory writes as well as FLOPs, and slice only when needed. Shape equality permits plan reuse, not numerical-environment reuse. |
| Chen et al., [Sign problem in tensor network contraction](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.19023), PRX Quantum 2025 | Abstract and full-text discussion of sign structure | Sign structure can change approximation difficulty. Its transformations for physical PEPS expectation/norm structures do not license taking absolute values of a general signed Pauli scalar network. |

A useful distinction: **block BP, overlapping-region GBP, loop corrections, matrix-product messages and contraction-tree messages are different algorithms**. Failure of plain BP does not dismiss all of them. Conversely, calling a boundary method “belief propagation” does not grant it the guarantees of positive graphical models.

## Compression, noisy evolution and alternative representations

| Source | Evidence inspected | What it suggests; what it does not establish |
|---|---|---|
| Unfried, Hauschild & Pollmann, [Fast Time-Evolution of Matrix-Product States using the QR decomposition](https://arxiv.org/abs/2212.09782), PRB 2023 | Abstract and previously reviewed algorithm sections | Controlled bond expansion with QR can avoid a large SVD. Our elementary nonunitary/noisy/unsigned steps need independent validation, including newly appearing charge sectors. |
| Kohn et al., [Probabilistic low-rank factorization accelerates tensor network simulations](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.01463), 2017 manuscript | Abstract | Supports low-rank factorizations when retained rank is small. Current code already has RSVD; the open question is when actual ranks justify it. |
| Zhang, Yang & Corboz, [Accelerating two-dimensional tensor network contractions using QR decompositions](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.00494), PRB 2026 | Abstract and method scope | QR-based CTMRG is relevant inspiration, but its spatial C4v setting does not follow from our internal Z2×Z2 symmetry. |
| Zhang & Corboz, [Fast two-dimensional tensor-network contraction via subspace iteration](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.15158), July 2026 preprint | Abstract | Recycle dominant subspaces and factor a smaller core. Finite, inhomogeneous messages and noise-scale changes can destroy reuse; monitor the unrepresented residual and expand the basis. |
| Ganahl et al., [Density Matrix Renormalization Group with Tensor Processing Units](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05693), PRX Quantum 2023 | Earlier method review and abstract | Matrix-multiplication-heavy polar methods can fit accelerators. Our simple Newton–Schulz experiment does not reproduce their stabilized implementation or very large TPU workload. |
| Fröhlich et al., [Noisy quantum circuit simulation with the tensor jump method](https://arxiv.org/abs/2607.01323), July 2026 preprint | Abstract | Sparse Pauli-Lindblad trajectories match our noise family and expose independent work. Sampling noise exactly does not remove MPS/TDVP truncation error. Small signed signals may need many trajectories; unsigned Q masses are not ordinary physical expectation values. |
| Angrisani et al., [Simulating quantum circuits with arbitrary local noise using Pauli Propagation](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.13101), PRX Quantum 2026 | Abstract, assumptions and metadata | Encourages operator/Pauli-weight truncation. Its average-case circuit assumptions and local-noise setting do not automatically cover deterministic Heisenberg layers with correlated weight-two noise. |
| Shao et al., [Complexity of tensor network simulation for noisy quantum circuits](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00474), 2026 preprint | Abstract and stated theorem regimes | Separates absolute from relative accuracy and specifies depolarizing/strong-contraction assumptions. Weak correlated noise does not by itself guarantee an easy boundary contraction. |
| [quimb 1D compression documentation](https://quimb.readthedocs.io/en/main/autoapi/quimb/tensor/tn1d/compress/index.html) | Official API descriptions | Density-matrix, zip-up and variational fitting are useful comparison methods. Forming a Gram matrix may worsen conditioning; compatibility with installed symmray blocks must be checked. The online development API is not assumed identical to installed quimb 1.14. |

For our next boundary-compression experiment, compare a guide-weighted retained subspace with ordinary Frobenius truncation. Keep the guide fixed during one update, evaluate the true residual of that update, and refresh guides through controlled sweeps. This is a proposed adaptation, not a result proved by the cited papers for our exact workflow.

## Apple and GPU sources that affect implementation choices

| Source | Evidence inspected | Consequence |
|---|---|---|
| Abdelfattah & Fasi, [An Efficient Batch Solver for the Singular Value Decomposition on GPUs](https://arxiv.org/html/2601.17979v2), 2026 preprint | Full text: blocked Jacobi, local Gram eigensolvers, masking, memory and benchmarks | Larger cores need block updates and tuned matrix products; local shared-memory eigensolvers are only part of the solver. Full AᵀA eigendecomposition is not equivalent numerically to a well-designed one-sided Jacobi method. |
| Boukaram et al., [Batched QR and SVD Algorithms on GPUs](https://arxiv.org/abs/1707.05141), 2017 manuscript | Abstract and prior review | Reinforces batch size, memory hierarchy and factorization-specific kernels. GPU peak FLOPs alone do not predict local-update performance. |
| Ringoot et al., [Performant Unified GPU Kernels for Portable Singular Value Computation Across Hardware and Precision](https://arxiv.org/html/2508.06339v1), ICPP 2025 | Full text: two-stage reductions, Apple support, conclusion | Metal support is real, but the paper's implementation computes singular values; vector accumulation is future work. Evaluate its QR/reduction ideas, not a presumed ready SVD backend. |
| [metal-linalg](https://github.com/iandriver/metal-linalg), author repository | README and stated limits | Small batched threadgroup Jacobi/Householder implementation and CPU routing. The inspected README's M5/batch-16,384 timings are not comparable to one M4 sector. Package not benchmarked here. |
| [metalcore](https://github.com/myfykris/metalcore), author repository | README and examples | Advertised Jacobi speedups target large LLM matrices; its example uses 4096×4096 and its override targets ≥512×512. Claims need reproduction on our shapes, spectra and required vectors. |
| [Apple vs. Oranges: Evaluating the Apple Silicon M-Series SoCs for HPC Performance and Efficiency](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.05317), 2025 manuscript | Abstract | Apple-specific CPU/GPU/unified-memory performance matters. Use Accelerate as a serious baseline and account for shared bandwidth rather than importing discrete-GPU crossover rules. |
| Kumaresan, [Rigel: Reverse-Engineering the Metal 4.1 Tensor Compute Path on the Apple M4 Max GPU](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.12765), June 2026 preprint | Abstract | Hardware/API feature exposure does not guarantee native acceleration. Its M4 Max FP8 observation is not a benchmark of our FP32 scientific kernels. |
| [Apple: running inline ML operations with Metal 4](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/metal/running-inline-ml-operations-in-a-shader-with-metal-4) | Official documentation | Cooperative tensor operations and fusion are worth investigating. Dedicated newer-family neural accelerators must not be attributed to our M4 Pro. |
| [Apple: creating threads and threadgroups](https://developer.apple.com/documentation/metal/creating-threads-and-threadgroups) | Official documentation | Tune execution width, threadgroup occupancy and shared memory; correct synchronization is part of performance design. |
| [MLX custom Metal kernels](https://ml-explore.github.io/mlx/build/html/dev/custom_metal_kernels.html) and [NumPy interoperability](https://ml-explore.github.io/mlx/build/html/usage/numpy.html) | Official docs and existing local prototype | Reuse buffers and fuse operations, while respecting array lifetime, synchronization and layout. Unified memory does not eliminate dtype conversion, packing or dependency barriers. |

The earlier GPU review covers symmetry-aware hybrid DMRG, contraction layout, distributed block sparsity, slicing, mixed precision and parallel TDVP. Those references remain relevant; their cluster hardware speedups are not evidence of laptop performance.

## Candidate map: which paths to pursue

| Path | Status in this project | Priority and next discriminating test |
|---|---|---|
| Row-level telescoping of residual overlaps | New prototype; same scalar, 1.14–1.41× faster stage | High, low numerical risk. Integrate without removing requested bond diagnostics. |
| Guide-weighted finite boundary compression | Literature/design | High algorithmic priority. Compare equal-time error against current residual correction and exact fixed-PEPS references. |
| Sector-adaptive rank choice / QR controlled expansion | Existing RSVD plus literature/design | High for expensive evolution. Preserve global rank selection and account for every approximation in MPS residuals. |
| Persistent CPU decomposition pools | New throughput benchmark | Use only where measured; this installed stack did not consistently benefit from more workers. |
| Independent CPU/GPU overlap | New throughput benchmark | Shape-specific. Add only after identifying useful ready work and measuring contention. |
| Small batched Jacobi with shared storage and deflation | New prototype and adversarial tests | Promising narrow regime; full nullspace/isometry and observable accuracy still block promotion. |
| Blocked Jacobi for medium cores | Design, not implemented | Highest custom-SVD kernel priority beyond small cores. Benchmark local eigensolves, vector updates and masking separately. |
| GPU range finding / CPU QR and core SVD | New benchmark | Useful at large size and small retained-rank fraction. FP32 range error still needs a acceptance criterion. |
| GPU polar / iterative canonicalization | New benchmark | Promising on conditioned large batches. Require orthogonality as well as reconstruction, with rank-deficiency handling. |
| Householder TSQR and compact-WY updates | Existing experimental GPU code | Audit/tune tall matrices; retain reflectors and apply them directly where explicit Q is unnecessary. |
| Tiled SIMD-group GEMM with fused output reductions | Scalar ragged prototype lost; tuned version unimplemented | Revisit for sufficiently large grouped requests; the negative scalar-kernel result does not settle a tiled design. |
| Fuse gauge/noise scaling and permutations with contraction | Partial CPU optimization; GPU design | Avoid standalone tiny kernels. Measure bytes packed and next-consumer layout, preserving elementary unsigned semantics. |
| Shared slabs and a bounded two-slot queue | Existing one-slot shared-buffer prototype; two slots unimplemented | Bound all live arrays, not just nominal inputs. Benchmark overlap against extra shared-memory bandwidth traffic. |
| Compile/replay repeated GPU graphs | Used in polar benchmark; broader executor unimplemented | Cache by shape/dtype/frame and refresh numerical inputs safely. Avoid stale shared views in retained lazy graphs. |
| Direct Metal 4 tensor primitives | Literature/design | Compare with installed MLX GEMM on M4, not M5 marketing or FP8 throughput. |
| Full stationary matrix-product BP | Literature; only Bethe functional tested | Medium/high. Requires stable finite non-Hermitian environment updates and useful accuracy at equal cost. |
| Contraction-tree boundary messages | Literature/design | Medium/high after finite-strip validation. Optimize separator ordering, memory and truncation together. |
| Plaquette GBP / larger block BP | Older signed block pilot failed; GBP not implemented | Lower priority than correlated boundary messages. Start with exact trees and signed small fixtures; convergence alone is insufficient. |
| Selected deterministic loop/cluster corrections | Literature/design | Medium research priority. Measure successive cluster contributions and cancellation; do not assume exponential decay. |
| Stochastic loop corrections | Literature/design | Exploratory. Quantify variance and mixing at the actual small signed observable scale. |
| Residual-guided adaptive boundary/guide caps | Design | High potential memory/accuracy gain. Spend rank near large environment-weighted errors; verify with independent cap increases. |
| Recycle bases across rows, steps or scales | Literature/design | Conditional. Reuse subspaces with expansion/refresh; do not freeze ranks through CNOT frame changes. |
| Contraction direction/tree selection and index slicing | Existing libraries, no new integrated backend | Use for anisotropy and memory limits. Charge-resolved slicing can expose independent work but may repeat substantial computation. |
| Noise-edge tables and graph-aware MPO ordering | Already implemented experimentally | Avoid rediscovering this optimization. Lower graph-frontier width and compress actual table spectra without changing the channel. |
| Observable causal cones and sparse Pauli tails | Existing related workspace work; further design | Useful structural complement. Establish which noise terms act trivially and preserve the exact backward operation order. |
| Pauli-jump trajectories / control variates | Literature/design | Separate stochastic alternative for signed observables. Evaluate total bias plus sampling error; no direct substitution for unsigned Q. |
| Low precision, compensated or emulated FP64 products | Prior literature; no new validated kernel | Use only with observable error targets. Splitting input mantissas alone does not fix FP32 accumulation error. |
| Infinite CTMRG/VUMPS imported wholesale | Literature considered | Low priority for our current finite, inhomogeneous signed scalar. Borrow compression ideas after checking assumptions. |

## How to judge an algorithm fairly

Keep three comparisons separate: identical arithmetic/schedule on a different device; a different factorization at the same retained rank; and a different contraction/compression algorithm at the same observable accuracy. Only the last determines the best scientific time-to-result.

The validation ladder is: exact tree fixtures; rank-deficient/ill-conditioned sector matrices; exact small fixed-PEPS contractions; signed and unsigned curves with valid ratio/Q denominators; larger cap/depth/graph convergence; then full runtime and peak memory. Charge leakage, zero sectors, weak/zero noise, cross-pair generators and long-range edges belong in the scientific tests. Synthetic positive matrices alone cannot establish viability.

An algorithm should earn promotion by reducing measured full-run cost at a stated accuracy. A converged BP residual, a fast GPU SVD reconstruction, and a small local discarded weight each answer a narrower question.
